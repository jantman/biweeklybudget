Cash Position

How much money you actually have that is not already spoken for. Every figure below is a term of the calculation reported by the banner at the top of every page; each one links to the view it comes from, so a number that looks wrong can be tracked down rather than just observed.

Current pay period: {{ cp.pay_period.start_date|dateymd }} to {{ cp.pay_period.end_date|dateymd }}. Account balances are as of the dates shown in the detail tables below; a ledger balance can lag reality by days.

+ Budget-funding account balances Ledger balance of every active bank and cash account. {{ cp.budget_account_ledger|reddollars|safe }}
− Adjustment for unreconciled transactions Transactions you have entered but not yet matched to a real bank transaction. Subtracting these turns the ledger balance above into the projected balance. Transactions that move no real cash are already excluded. {{ cp.unreconciled|reddollars|safe }}
+ Outstanding credit account balances Money owed on a card is recorded as a negative balance, so adding this subtracts what you owe. A card carrying a positive balance — overpaid, or holding a statement credit larger than its balance — really does hold money you can spend, and correctly raises the figure below. {{ cp.credit_balance|reddollars|safe }}
= Net liquid position {{ cp.net_liquid|reddollars|safe }}
− Standing budget balances Money carried over from period to period in your active standing budgets. It is money you hold, but it is already assigned. {{ cp.standing_total|reddollars|safe }}
− Current pay period: allocated but not yet spent Allocated minus spent for the pay period containing today. This is not the pay period view's own “remaining” figure, which is income minus budgeted; the two answer different questions and routinely disagree. {{ cp.pay_period_allocated_unspent|reddollars|safe }}
= Truly unallocated / uncommitted funds {{ cp.uncommitted|reddollars|safe }}

Standing budgets against the accounts holding them

A budget's money can be spread across several accounts, and one account commonly holds several earmarked budgets.
